Satisfactory Plus

Satisfactory Plus replaces nearly every recipe in the game. This page is the way in: what is different, the order to do things in, six builds drawn machine by machine that take you to the first Space Elevator delivery, and every recipe behind a search box for when you want it.

The mods that go with it are the Howson Plus pack. The Rocky Desert Works and the Builder's Handbook are for the shared server as it runs today, on the base game's recipes; nothing here applies there until the server restarts on the Plus pack.

What is different

  • The ores are minerals with new names. Siterite holds iron and tin, Callanite copper and magnesium, Larrussite zinc and silica. Later come Aurovite (caterium), Montanion (cobalt and aluminium) and Kyalite (quartz and salt). In the game data they are the base game's iron, copper, limestone, caterium, bauxite and quartz nodes under new names, so the map you know still applies.
  • Ore is crushed before it is smelted, by hand at first, then in the Crusher. From Tier 1 the Sorter splits the crushed ore further: 24 Crushed Siterite become 18 Crushed Iron, 12 Crushed Tin and 8 Crushed Gangue.
  • Concrete is made from Crushed Gangue, the stone that crushing and sorting leave behind. Three make one Concrete.
  • Tin and bronze come before steel. Tin Plates arrive at HUB Upgrade 4, bronze in Tier 2; steel waits for .
  • The miner is modular. A Modular Miner needs a Mining Head: the Standard head gives 30 a minute from each of two outputs, the Hardened head 60, the Diamond head 120. An impure node halves that, a pure node doubles it.
  • No Splitters or Mergers until Tier 1's Logistics Mk. 2. The first lines are straight: one belt, one machine after another.
  • The first power you build is the Biomass Burner (30 MW) or the Wind Turbine Mk1, both at HUB Upgrade 6. The turbine gives 20 to 60 MW depending on how high it stands, and less when other turbines are close.
  • The Space Elevator has a price of its own: 25 Bronze Frame, 75 Modular Frame, 200 Tin Plate, 500 Concrete and 500 Screws. Its first delivery is 75 Smart Plating and 25 Modular Frame.
  • The early game is hand-crafted more than the base game. Tin Plates and Reinforced Iron Plates have no machine until the Assembler in Tier 1.

The builds

Blueprint style: no map, just the machines, what goes in and what comes out, so they work wherever your world starts. Click any block for the detail. Numbers after a part are per minute; an arrow points to the block it goes to.

Build 1 · Opening yard, Siterite

Your first automatic lines. A Modular Miner has two outputs, and until Splitters arrive each output gets a line of its own: a Crusher, a Smelter, and a Constructor that turns the crushed stone into Concrete.

  • Fit the Standard Mining Head to the miner first; it is a separate item in the build menu.
  • Each Crusher has one output for the crushed ore and one for the Crushed Gangue. Run the gangue to its Constructor and end each line in a Storage Container.
  • Both Crushers are set to 50%: each sees only one of the miner's two outputs.

Build 3 · Siterite yard

Build 6 · Platform works

Makes the three parts that the Space Elevator and its first delivery ask for. It has its own pair of yards, built exactly like the first pair, so the Parts hall keeps running.

  • Run it for 33 minutes and you have the 75 Smart Plating, 100 Modular Frames and 25 Bronze Frames that the Space Elevator and its first delivery need together.
  • The Space Elevator also costs 200 Tin Plate, 500 Concrete and 500 Screws; those come out of the Parts hall's containers and the yards' Concrete.
  • Several machines run well under 100%. That is deliberate: every part is made at exactly the rate the next machine uses it, so nothing piles up.

What is checked and what is not

  • Checked by the page builder, every time it runs: each step of the path can be paid for with recipes unlocked before it; each machine in a build is unlocked by then; each build makes exactly what it says with nothing left over; no belt carries more than its tier allows; the builds before Logistics Mk. 2 need no Splitter or Merger; the yards cover what the halls take.
  • Where the numbers come from: the game data that the mod's authors publish on their wiki at k-mods.com, exported 2026-08-30 and read 2026-11-14: 580 recipes, 288 machine behaviours, 401 parts.
  • Not checked: nothing here has been built in the game. The wiki's export may be older or newer than the version you have installed. Machine footprints are the game's clearance boxes, so the floor sizes are estimates. Where the input and output ports sit on a machine is not in the data.
  • Assumed: that a Crusher and a Sorter have a separate output for each product (their descriptions say the output order is shown in the machine's menu), and that a normal node is the worst you will build on.
