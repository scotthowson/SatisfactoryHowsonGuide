Rocky Desert Works

Base round the Rocky Desert Lake. A camp between two pure iron nodes, a works of three halls across a 16 m street from it, a storage depot and a plaza for the HUB along that street, a power station on a terrace over the lake, steel and concrete on the plain behind it, the later halls straight across the water, and an oil yard on the coast. Every pad has room round it to become a building.

How the base is laid out

  • Main Street, 16 m wide and never built on. Its north half carries every belt on one rack; its south half stays clear to walk and drive, with the hypertube overhead later.
  • The Works, north of the street: Hall 1 for Reinforced Iron Plates and Smart Plating, Hall 2 for copper, Hall 3 for the plates and rods you build with, and the Space Elevator on its own pad.
  • The Camp, south of the street: the storage depot with one bay for each part, the HUB plaza and its terrace with every bench at its real size, the pure iron node Iron A, and the biofuel depot.
  • The Lake Bank, phase 2: the power station where you asked for it, as a terrace over the bank, with four Water Extractors in the lake off its corner.
  • The West Plain, south of the station: the Concrete works in phase 1 and the Steel Works in phase 2.
  • The North Shore, phases 2 and 3: two blocks of clear ground across the lake, kept empty and reached by a causeway.
  • The Oil Coast, phase 3: an oil yard beside a pure oil node, 1240 m to the south-west, on its own map.
  • First foundation: north-west corner at -1688, -320. It is the south-east foundation of Hall 1.

How a storage bay works

  • A container's belt ports are at its two ends. One end takes a belt in, the other gives one out; the long sides are blank. So each container stands end-on to the street: inlet north, outlet south.
  • One bay, one part, two containers. A bay is one foundation wide. The second container stands on the first; both hold the same part, 48 stacks in all.
  • One Splitter per bay. The belt comes down from the rack into a Splitter at the street end. Its straight outlet goes into the bottom container; a side outlet feeds a Conveyor Lift up to the top one.
  • Why not a tree of Splitters? A tree shares a belt evenly between machines that must all run at once. A store only has to fill: whichever container has room takes the parts, and when both are full the belt backs up and the hall behind it rests. One Splitter does that.
  • Getting parts out. Walk the south side and open any container: you can take from it by hand from any side. When a part has to travel on by belt, to the Space Elevator or a truck station, hook the belt to the south end.

How a belt gets there

  • Every belt rides the rack. One row of Stackable Conveyor Poles down the street's north half, from Tier 2, Logistics Mk.2. Before that, the first belts run on ordinary Conveyor Poles along the same line.
  • It leaves opposite its bay, crosses the street overhead, and comes down to the bay's Splitter.
  • Nothing crosses on the ground, so a new part never means moving an old belt: it takes the next height on the rack.

Start with eight

One pure coal node with a Miner Mk.1 gives 120 coal a minute, which a Mk.2 belt carries. That feeds exactly eight generators, and eight generators drink exactly three extractors. So build the four western pairs and E1 to E3 first: 600 MW.

Add G5, G10 and E4 when Logistics Mk.3 lets one belt carry more and the Miner Mk.2 lets the node give it.

A terrace, not a floor on the ground

The ground under it falls about 8 m from south to north. Lay the first row of foundations along its south edge, level with Main Street, and build north from there; the floor carries on level over the bank. Pillars under the north half are for looks: foundations do not need them.

Water goes uphill

The terrace stands well above the lake and a Water Extractor lifts water only 10 m by itself. One Pipeline Pump Mk.1 on each of the two pipes, at the shore, adds 20 m. Pumps are unlocked with Coal Power, the same milestone as the generators.

Two pipes, not one: a Mk.1 pipe carries 300 m³ a minute and four extractors give 480. Both run straight south from the extractors down the lane east of the station and turn into the aisle; the longer is about 140 m.

Power home

Power Towers are Tier 4. At Tier 3, run a row of Power Poles along the north edge of Main Street, about every 40 m. At Tier 4 the two towers on the plan replace it with one line of 174 m.

Why coke and a Sink

Plastic and Rubber both leave Heavy Oil Residue behind. If it has nowhere to go, the Refineries stop. Two more Refineries turn it into Petroleum Coke and an AWESOME Sink eats the coke, so nothing can back up, and the Sink pays coupons.

Fuel power, later

The fuel power yard is kept clear beside the normal node. Its oil stays on its own pipe: a generator burns less when the grid needs less, and that must never hold up the Plastic and Rubber.

Room for what comes later

Hypertubes, Tier 4

  • The line is on the site plan in purple: overhead along the south edge of Main Street, up the service lane, round the station's corner and along the causeway to the North Shore.
  • Four entrances are marked: beside the plaza terrace, at the Works, at the service lane and on the North Shore.
  • Until Tier 4, build nothing on that line. That is all it asks.

The service lane

  • 16 m of bare ground east of the power station and the Steel Works, at x = -1868. Everything that goes to or from the west runs down it: two water pipes, coal, iron ore, steel, concrete.
  • Each has its own line in the lane, 3 m apart, so they never cross on the ground. Where one must cross another, raise it one step on Stackable Conveyor Poles.

Belts faster than Mk.1

  • Every hall is drawn for Mk.1 and Mk.2 belts. When Mk.3 arrives, the same floors take twice the machines: the gaps between rows are there for that.

The short version

  • Two settings do most of the work: cap hand crafting, and hold Industrial Evolution back until phase 2 is delivered.
  • Create the world with the game's normal settings. Advanced Game Settings stay off on the server.
  • Nothing here is measured. I have read each mod's own page and its settings file, not timed a playthrough.

Can we use blueprints from the start?

  • Not the downloaded ones. The base game unlocks the Blueprint Designer at Tier 4. The repository holds 194 blueprints; the 15 picked for the early game still need Mk.2 belts or better, all but one.
  • The ImKibitz packs are for later. 179 blueprints made in the Tier 6 and Tier 9 designers. 87 have no belts in them: roads, platforms, walls, walkways and pipe runs. Most of the rest use Mk.5 or Mk.6 belts.
  • Small ones, early. The pack's Progressive Blueprinters adds designers 1, 2 and 3 foundations across. The first asks you to gather a full stack of every basic material.
  • The handbook is the early blueprint. Its eleven modules are drawn to be built by hand from the first hour, anywhere.

What I checked, and what I did not

Checked

  • Every floor square against the Satisfactory Calculator map at its deepest zoom, 4.4 pixels to the metre, in three surveys: home, the oil coast, and the ground between. The page will not build if a floor other than the power station touches a square with a contour, a rock edge or water in it, if two floors are closer than 8 m, if a floor stands in Main Street or the service lane, or if an extractor is not wholly over deep water.
  • The map's position formula, against a pond whose place was confirmed in the game in the Grass Fields.
  • Node positions and heights against the map's data file. 10 map heights on and beside the flat home floors read -7.7 to -5 m.
  • The 22 Lithium nodes the mod adds, read from the mod's own source: none is under a floor. The nearest is 230 m from Hall 1.
  • Every Power Tower stands on clear ground, and no span is over 264 m on the map.
  • Each hall's line fits its floor exactly, no machine or pole overlaps another, the recipe arithmetic holds, and every Splitter and Merger sits on a belt.
  • On the wiki, on 13 Nov 2026: what each Space Elevator phase asks for and unlocks; what every HUB upgrade and every Tier 1 and Tier 2 milestone unlocks; the tiers of Power Towers, Hypertubes, the Miner Mk.2, the belt marks, Oil Processing and Petroleum Power; that Smart Plating cannot be hand-crafted and a Rotor can; and the sizes of the HUB, the Space Elevator, the Storage Container, the Craft Bench, the Equipment Workshop, the MAM, the AWESOME Sink and the AWESOME Shop.
  • Where a Storage Container's belt ports are: read from real blueprints in the repository, where belts join a container 4 m from its centre along its length, at both ends.
  • The page itself in a browser: zoom, drag, phases, cards, layers, legend and ticks.

Not checked in the game

  • Nobody has stood on this ground. The map is a drawing of the world, not the world. If rock taller than you stands inside a floor outline, stop and tell me the coordinates.
  • The power station's bank. The map says it falls about 8 m and has a rock edge along its north-west corner; I have reasoned that the edge is the lip of the drop, not rock that rises.
  • Where you land. The game drops you somewhere in the Rocky Desert start area; the walk to the lake is not measured.
  • That four Water Extractors place where drawn. Test them before laying the station floor.
  • The route of the coal belt down the plateau and across the ridge, and the copper belt's way over it.
  • The two North Shore blocks are clear on the map, with one map height beside each: -0.8 and -3.2 m, so 3 to 5 m above the home plain.
  • The causeway is a line on the map, not a design. 112 m of it is over the lake and no rock edge is on it.
  • The oil coast and the tower line. The beach reads -16.8 m at the one map height on it; heights along the line come from the nearest map marker, up to 120 m away.
  • Which way the HUB's door, the Sink's inlet and the benches face. Everything on the plaza has room on all sides for that reason.
  • The phase 2 and phase 3 halls are budgets, not drawings: the rates are recipe arithmetic, and the Adaptive Control Unit's recipe is from the Oil Lake plan, not re-read.
