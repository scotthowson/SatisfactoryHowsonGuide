First jobs

Four short jobs for whoever is new to the game: a small yard of your own that makes what every floor of the base is made of. Each job leaves something running, and nothing you build has to be moved later.

Written by tools/firstjobs.py from the plan itself; the Satisfactory Plus guide and the World map carry the same steps with tick boxes.

The game in eight lines

  • Node and miner. A node is a patch of ore in the ground. A miner stands on it and brings ore up without end.
  • Belt. A belt carries things one way, from one machine's outlet to the next machine's inlet.
  • Machine and recipe. A machine turns one thing into another. What it makes is its recipe: walk up to it, press E and choose.
  • Power. Machines need power: something that makes it, a Power Pole, and a Power Line from the pole to each machine. A Power Pole Mk.1 takes four lines.
  • Storage Container. A box with a belt inlet and an outlet: the end of a line. When it is full the line behind it stops and waits. Nothing breaks.
  • The build gun. Q opens the build menu. Pick a piece, turn it with the mouse wheel, click to set it down. F takes things down again, and everything comes back into your pockets.
  • Parts. Everything you build costs parts out of your pockets. On the World map, What it takes in the Plan panel lists them for each job, beside what the base's boxes hold.
  • Crushing. In this pack ore is crushed before it is smelted, and the Crusher also gives stone, Crushed Gangue. Concrete is made of that.

The yard

Everything we build stands on Foundations, the 8 m squares of floor. One Foundation costs 2 Concrete, 1 Iron Sheet, 2 Iron Plate. The yard makes exactly those three, on a Siterite node that nothing else uses, at -1525, -458: about 90 m east of the ground kept for the Parts hall, on a sand terrace between the lake and a rock ridge. No hall of the plan makes Iron Sheets before Tiers 3 and 4, so until then these are the base's sheets.

The yard stands on a floor of its own: 28 Foundations, which is the first thing you lay. The miner stands on the ground just north of it, and the floor's west half stays free for a second line later.

The lighter squares are the floor. The number in a corner is the job a piece comes with. Orange lines are belts, with an arrow where the parts arrive; purple dots are Power Poles A, B and C, and the dashed lines their Power Lines. The grid is the world's 8 m grid; the corners carry game coordinates.

Before you start

  • Open the World map. In its Plan panel, What it takes lists the parts for Foundation yard, or for one job at a time, beside what the base's boxes hold. What is short you make by hand at a Crafting Bench (the two Portable Miners of job 1 at an Equipment Workshop), or ask Scott.
  • In the map's Guide panel the jobs are a list with tick boxes; Show flies the map to a step and Navigate gives the game a beam to walk to.
  • Under Shown in your game in the same panel, Plan outlines draws each planned piece on the ground where it goes. It needs the SatMap mod in your game.
  • Belts and Power Lines are paid for by length as you lay them and are not in those lists: Iron Plates and Screws for belt, Cable for line. Take 100 Iron Plate, 100 Screws and 15 Cable for all three jobs; the last section says where that number comes from.

Job 1: Ore to ingots

28 Foundation (1 m), 3 Power Pole Mk.1, 2 Storage Container, 1 Biomass Burner, 1 Crusher, 1 Modular Miner, 1 Smelter, 1 Standard Mining Head. They cost 86 Iron Plate, 76 Concrete, 71 Screws, 37 Iron Rod, 28 Iron Sheet, 22 Cable, 17 Wire, 10 Tin Plate, 5 Iron Wire, 4 Copper Busbars, 3 Copper Rod, 2 Portable Miner. About 69 m of belt. Of that the floor alone is 56 Concrete, 28 Iron Sheet, 56 Iron Plate.

  1. Go to the spare Siterite node at -1525, -458. A node is a patch of ore in the ground. This one is Siterite, the pack's iron ore, and nothing else uses it. It lies on a sand terrace east of the lake, about 90 m east of the ground kept for the Parts hall and 13 m above the camp, with a rock ridge behind it. Press Show, then Navigate, and the game gets a beam to walk to; a band of rock lies between the Parts hall's ground and the terrace, and the way over it is yours to find. Before you go, open What it takes in the map's Plan panel and pick Foundation yard, Job 1: it lists every part the job costs beside what the base's boxes hold. Nobody has walked this ground yet: the map's picture shows open sand south of the node. On the map
  2. Lay the floor: 6 by 4 Foundations from -1568, -448, and 4 more along its south edge. A Foundation is an 8 m square of floor: machines stand level on it and are easy to line up. Set the first one down at the north-west corner, -1568, -448, with Ctrl held, so that it sits on the world's grid as the map draws it; the others snap to it. With a Foundation in hand, R switches to Zoop, which lays a whole row at a time. Six across and four down, then four more along the south edge from -1552, -416. The node stays off the floor, 10 m north of its north edge. Lay it level: where the sand dips the pieces stand in the air, and that is fine. Rock lies along the floor's west edge and south of its south-west corner; if rock comes up through a Foundation, tell Scott where. A dark spiky plant about 3 m across stands where the floor goes, at -1539.5, -437.8: it should disappear under the Foundation, and if it does not, say so before you set the Crusher down beside it. 28 Foundations cost 56 Concrete, 28 Iron Sheet, 56 Iron Plate. On the map
  3. The Modular Miner on the node, its two outlets facing south. A miner stands on a node and brings ore up without end. Open the build menu (Q), take the Modular Miner, aim at the node and turn it with the mouse wheel until its two belt outlets point south, toward the floor; click to set it down. Then the Standard Mining Head, a second piece in the build menu, onto the miner: without a head it does nothing. On this node each outlet gives 60 ore a minute. The yard uses the west one, at -1527, -454; leave the other empty. Miner and head cost 10 Iron Plate, 5 Cable, 10 Tin Plate, 10 Concrete, 45 Screws, 2 Portable Miner, 15 Iron Rod. On the map
  4. The Crusher, and a belt from the miner. In this pack ore is crushed before it is smelted. The Crusher goes on the floor's north edge, south-west of the miner, its middle at -1535.5, -442, turned so that its two inlets face east; it is 11 by 10 m. Then a Conveyor Belt Mk.1 from the miner's west outlet 10 m south and round into the Crusher's north inlet, at -1532, -444: take the belt in the build menu, click the outlet, then the inlet. A belt carries things one way, from an outlet to an inlet, and bends by itself where it has to turn. The Crusher costs 5 Iron Wire, 5 Cable, 10 Screws, 10 Concrete. On the map
  5. The Smelter, south-west of the Crusher. A Smelter melts crushed ore into ingots. Its middle at -1542, -431, inlet to the north. A belt from the Crusher's south outlet, at -1540, -440, a step west and then 6 m south into it. The Crusher has two outlets because it makes two things: crushed ore, which should leave by the south one, and stone (Crushed Gangue), by the north one. The Smelter costs 5 Wire, 5 Iron Plate, 2 Cable, 2 Iron Rod. On the map
  6. Two Storage Containers, and the two long belts. A Storage Container is a box with a belt inlet at one end and an outlet at the other: a line ends in one. The stone's box goes in the floor's north-west corner, its middle at -1561, -444 and its inlet facing east; the ingots' box near the south-east corner, at -1534, -420, inlet facing south. Stone belt: from the Crusher's north outlet, at -1540, -444, straight west along the floor's north edge into its box, 17 m. Ingot belt: from the Smelter's outlet 17 m south, 8 m east, then north into its box. The map draws both. They are long for what they do today: job 2 sets a machine into each. Two boxes cost 10 Screws, 10 Iron Rod, 10 Iron Plate. On the map
  7. Power: a Biomass Burner, three Power Poles and six lines. Machines stand still without power. The Biomass Burner goes in the floor's west half, its middle at -1556, -433, its belt inlet to the south. A Power Pole Mk.1 takes four Power Lines. Pole A at -1549, -438: a line to the burner. Pole B at -1537, -431: a line from pole A, and one to the Smelter. Pole C at -1530, -431: a line from pole B, one to the Crusher and one to the miner. To lay a line, take Power Line in the build menu, click one end, then the other. Burner and poles cost 5 Iron Plate, 10 Iron Rod, 10 Cable, 4 Copper Busbars, 12 Wire, 6 Screws, 3 Copper Rod; each line costs Cable by its length. On the map
  8. Fuel, recipes, and watch it run. Walk up to the burner, press E and put Biomass, Wood or Leaves in its place for fuel. Biomass, made from Leaves or Wood at a Crafting Bench, lasts longest: a stack of 200 runs this job for about 57 minutes. Then tell each machine what to make, which the game calls its recipe: E on the Crusher, Crushed Siterite; E on the Smelter, Impure Iron Ingot. Ore rides down the belt and soon the first Iron Ingots drop into the box, 30 a minute, with 12.5 Crushed Gangue a minute in the other. When a box is full the line behind it stops and waits; nothing breaks. Take the ingots along when you pass: the Crafting Bench and Scott's halls use them. On the map

Job 2: Plates and Concrete

2 Constructor, 1 Storage Container. They cost 25 Screws, 15 Iron Plate, 5 Iron Rod, 4 Cable. About 26 m of belt.

  1. A Constructor in the ingot belt: Iron Plate. A Constructor makes one part out of another. Take up the first piece of the ingot belt, from the Smelter down to its corner: press F, aim at the belt and hold the mouse button. Everything you take down comes back into your pockets. Set a Constructor with its middle at -1542, -420, inlet to the north. A belt from the Smelter into it, a belt from its outlet down to the corner, and a Power Line from pole B. E on it: Iron Plate. 30 ingots a minute now become 20 Iron Plates, and the box fills with plates: take the ingots out of it first. Costs 5 Iron Plate, 2 Cable, 10 Screws. On the map
  2. A Constructor in the stone belt: Concrete. Take up the stone belt. Set a Constructor with its middle at -1549, -444, turned so that its inlet faces east, toward the Crusher; a belt from the Crusher's north outlet into it, and a belt from its outlet on into the box. A Power Line from pole A. E on it: Concrete. Three stone make one Concrete: 4.17 a minute. Costs 5 Iron Plate, 2 Cable, 10 Screws. On the map
  3. A fuel box for the burner. So that nobody has to feed the burner by hand: a Storage Container south of it, its middle at -1556, -422 and its outlet facing the burner, and a belt of 4 m into the burner's inlet. Fill it with Biomass. Its 24 places full run the whole yard for about 11 hours. Costs 5 Screws, 5 Iron Rod, 5 Iron Plate. On the map

Job 3: A Splitter, and Iron Sheets

1 Constructor, 1 Conveyor Splitter, 1 Storage Container. They cost 20 Screws, 15 Iron Plate, 5 Iron Rod, 2 Cable, 2 Iron Sheet. About 21 m of belt.

  1. A Conveyor Splitter under the plate box. A Splitter takes one belt in and shares it out between up to three. Take up the short belt that runs north into the plate box. Set a Conveyor Splitter where it turned, at -1534, -412, its inlet toward the west, where the plates come from; if the belt from the west does not snap onto it, take that belt up and lay it again into the Splitter. Then a belt from the Splitter's north outlet into the plate box. A Conveyor Merger is the same piece the other way round: three belts in, one out. Scott's halls are full of both. Costs 5 Iron Plate, 2 Iron Sheet, 5 Screws. On the map
  2. A Constructor for Iron Sheets, and its box. A Constructor with its middle at -1526, -419, turned so that its inlet faces south. A belt from the Splitter's east outlet 8 m east and north into it; E on it: Iron Sheet. A Storage Container north of it, its middle at -1526, -431, and a belt between them. A Power Line from pole C. Half the plates now go into the box and half become sheets: 10 Iron Plates, 12.5 Iron Sheets and 4.17 Concrete a minute. Those three are what a Foundation costs: 2 Concrete, 1 Iron Sheet, 2 Iron Plate. The yard makes 2.1 Foundations' worth a minute; the Concrete sets the pace. Costs 10 Iron Plate, 2 Cable, 15 Screws, 5 Iron Rod. On the map

Job 4: Lay a floor with what you made

  1. Lay the Parts hall's floor: 6 by 8 Foundations from -1648, -440. The next hall of the build order stands here, 90 m west of your yard, and on 16 Nov it had no floor yet. 48 Foundations cost 96 Concrete, 48 Iron Sheet, 96 Iron Plate: your three boxes, about 23 minutes of the yard's Concrete. You know how from your own floor: the first one at the north-west corner with Ctrl held, then Zoop. Lay it level; if rock comes up through the floor, tell Scott where. On the map

After the four jobs

  • Keep the three boxes moving. The Lake Bank terrace, the next floor of the build order, is 100 Foundations: 200 Concrete, 100 Iron Sheet, 200 Iron Plate. Each deck of the Lake Works is 285.
  • Twice as much. The miner's east outlet is free, the Crusher works half the time, and the floor's west half is empty but for the burner. A second Smelter and a second Iron Plate Constructor would double the iron and the Concrete. Switch on Edit the plan on the World map and lay it out yourself: the editor knows every machine's size and ports.
  • A Wind Turbine in place of the burner. It needs no fuel and gives 20 to 60 MW depending on how high it stands, which nobody has measured here. It costs 5 Rotor, 15 Iron Rod, 20 Tin Plate, 50 Screws, so it waits for Rotors.
  • A house of your own. The east house of the Twin Houses, on the Grass Fields, is yours: its track is further down this guide.

What is known and what is not

  • Read from the game: every machine's size and where its inlets, outlets and power connector sit (the server's own catalogue, 16 Nov 2026); every cost; that all of it is unlocked in your world; the recipes and what they give a minute.
  • Read from Scott's own Crusher, one of them: that crushed ore leaves by the south outlet and stone by the north when the inlets face east.
  • From the map's picture and the survey only: the ground. Open sand under the floor, a slab of rock with boulders along its west edge and another south of its south-west corner, a rock ridge to the south-east, and under the floor a small rock and a dark spiky plant about 3 m across at -1539.5, -437.8. Nobody has walked it; how level it is, and whether the plant goes when a Foundation is laid over it, has to be seen.
  • Not known: what a belt and a Power Line cost by the metre here; the build gun shows it as you drag. The base game charges a belt's price once for every 2 m and a line's once for every 25 m. If this pack keeps that, the three jobs need about 60 Iron Plate, 60 Screws and 10 Cable for them: take 100, 100 and 15.
